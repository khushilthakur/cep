About The CEP Project

An empirical community engagement study applying computational thinking, GIS web mapping, and survey statistics to improve child pedestrian safety around Mumbai schools.

Curriculum Mandate

University of Mumbai CEP Framework

The Community Engagement Project (CEP) is a compulsory curriculum component for B.Sc. Computer Science Semester V under the University of Mumbai. The objective of this project is to take computer science beyond the traditional laboratory and apply software engineering, spatial data analysis, and empirical methodologies to tangible civic and community challenges.

Rather than producing a generic awareness website, this project implements a rigorous, data-driven pipeline: conducting physical field inspections during active school hours, gathering primary community survey feedback, evaluating infrastructure against IRC (Indian Roads Congress) school-zone guidelines, and generating an interactive open-source GIS risk map with actionable engineering recommendations.

Core Directives

Project Objectives

Nine key empirical and computational goals formulated for this community investigation.

Objective 01

Traffic Flow & Speed Analysis

Quantify vehicular volumes, modal composition, and turning speeds along school approach lanes during peak arrival (07:15–08:00 AM) and departure (01:15–02:15 PM) windows.

Objective 02

Pedestrian Infrastructure Audit

Survey continuous walkway dimensions, paving conditions, encroachment levels, kerb ramp accessibility, and physical barrier adequacy along student walking corridors.

Objective 03

Zebra Crossings & Signals

Assess visibility, stripe retro-reflectivity, signal phase durations, and driver yield behavior at designated pedestrian crossing points within 500m of the school.

Objective 04

Regulatory Signage & Markings

Inventory IRC standard signs ("School Zone Ahead", "Speed Limit 25 km/h", "No Parking"), checking for visibility, obstruction by foliage, and night-time retro-reflection.

Objective 05

Drop-off Conflict Identification

Map vehicle holding patterns, double-parking friction points, auto-rickshaw queue bottlenecks, and school bus halting behaviors directly in front of school gates.

Objective 06

Community Stakeholder Survey

Administer structured 14-question questionnaires to capture lived commute experiences, perceived threat levels, and suggestions from students, parents, and teachers.

Objective 07

Statistical Data Analysis

Process primary survey datasets using JavaScript and Chart.js to identify modal dependencies, travel time patterns, and statistically significant hazard perceptions.

Objective 08

Interactive GIS Risk Mapping

Plot georeferenced risk coordinates on open-source OpenStreetMap layers with interactive popups, photographic evidence, and multi-tier risk filtering.

Objective 09

Evidence-Based Solutions

Formulate 4-pillar recommendations covering civil road engineering, traffic management, school gate administrative policies, and civic safety awareness.

Selected Case Study

Study Area Profile

Geographic context and urban road typology of the surveyed educational institution.

St. Jude Model High School

Located along the bustling Swami Vivekananda Road arterial corridor in Vile Parle West, Mumbai (K-West Ward). The school accommodates over 1,450 students from pre-primary through secondary grades.

GPS Coordinates: 19.1028° N, 72.8430° E
Study Buffer: 500 Meters Radius
Major Arterial: S.V. Road (6 Lanes)
Transit Proximity: Vile Parle Station (450m)
Inspect Study Perimeter on Map 🗺️

Main Gate Intersection

Primary bottleneck where school buses, private cars, auto-rickshaws, and over 600 dispersing students converge simultaneously.

Station Road Junction

Major throughway with heavy auto-rickshaw turning movements and complete absence of pedestrian countdown signals.

North Perimeter Bus Halt

High-volume transit stop where broken pavers and informal hawker stalls force students into the carriage-way.

Community Engagement

Key Stakeholders Involved

Community partners and municipal authorities consulted during the field study.

🎒

Students

Primary vulnerable road users. Participated in surveys regarding subjective fear, crossing wait times, and travel difficulties.

👨‍👩‍👧

Parents & Guardians

Provided key insights into drop-off parking challenges, auto-rickshaw operations, and lack of designated pedestrian waiting zones.

👩‍🏫

School Teachers & Staff

Assisted in organizing student surveys and documented peak-time gate discipline and vehicle queue delays.

🏘️

Local Residents & Hawkers

Shared year-round observations of traffic gridlock, monsoon drainage hazards, and residential lane rat-running.

👮

Mumbai Traffic Police

Local traffic division personnel consulted on speed enforcement feasibility, warden deployments, and signal phases.

🏛️

BMC Ward Office

Recipient of engineering recommendations for footpath slab rehabilitation, road markings, and bollard installation.

Academic Transparency Notice

Replacing Demo Data With Actual Field Data

This website currently loads realistic demonstration baseline records so that mapping, chart visualisations, and navigation can be evaluated during initial stages. For final University submission and viva voce, you can easily plug in your actual fieldwork findings:

1. Update School & Risk Data: Open data/risk-data.json and replace the coordinates, school name, and risk locations with your actual field notes.
2. Update Survey Data: Open data/survey-data.json or use the on-site Survey page to record live responses from respondents.
3. Swap Photographs: Place your actual high-resolution field photos into assets/images/ (e.g. risk-r01.jpg) and update file paths in risk-data.json.
