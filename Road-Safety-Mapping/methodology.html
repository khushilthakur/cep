CEP Empirical Methodology

A systematic 15-stage workflow integrating on-ground community engagement, spatial data science, and empirical risk scoring.

Scientific Rigour

Data Triangulation Architecture

Ensuring objective, validated conclusions by synthesizing three independent research pillars.

🔍

1. Physical Field Observation

Direct physical inspection of roadway geometry, vehicle approach speeds, driver turning behavior, footpath encumbrances, and photographic evidence logging during peak school operational hours.

📝

2. Stakeholder Community Surveys

Primary qualitative and quantitative feedback from 50+ students, parents, educators, and local commuters regarding subjective danger perception, commute bottlenecks, and near-miss occurrences.

🗺️

3. Spatial GIS Risk Mapping

Georeferencing all audited spots using GPS coordinates, overlaying 500-meter school buffer zones, classifying hazard severities, and visualising spatial clusters via interactive open web mapping.

Project Lifecycle

15-Stage CEP Research Timeline

The chronological progression of our Community Engagement Project from inception to final university examination.

01
Phase I: Reconnaissance

School Selection & Consultation

Identified St. Jude Model High School, Vile Parle (W) due to its high-density urban arterial frontage and high student walkability share.

02
Phase I: Reconnaissance

Study Area Delineation

Established a 500-meter pedestrian buffer zone around the school campus incorporating all feeder roads, bus halts, and intersections.

03
Phase II: Fieldwork

On-Site Field Reconnaissance

Conducted initial site walkthroughs to map school gates, perimeter boundary walls, transit stops, and pedestrian desire lines.

04
Phase II: Fieldwork

Traffic Volume & Speed Spot-Checks

Observed vehicular volumes and approach speeds during peak morning arrival (07:15–08:00 AM) and afternoon dispersal (01:15–02:15 PM).

05
Phase II: Fieldwork

Infrastructure & Sidewalk Audit

Measured sidewalk widths, logged pavement cracks, noted utility obstructions, checked zebra stripe contrast, and inventoried warning signage.

06
Phase III: Community Engagement

Survey Instrument Formulation

Drafted a 14-question structured survey instrument covering modal split, commute times, subjective fear, and specific hazard locations.

07
Phase III: Community Engagement

Interviews & Stakeholder Dialogue

Conducted semi-structured interviews with school administrators, parent carpool drivers, auto-rickshaw operators, and local shopkeepers.

08
Phase III: Community Engagement

Primary Survey Data Collection

Collected 50 primary responses across students, teachers, and guardians to create an empirical community baseline dataset.

09
Phase IV: Computational Analysis

Quantitative Statistical Analysis

Calculated modal percentages, cross-tabulated commute durations against fear ratings, and computed percentage hazard frequencies using JavaScript.

10
Phase IV: Computational Analysis

Spatial Hazard Identification

Correlated participant-reported dangerous spots with physical field observation notes to isolate 6 specific vulnerability hotspots (R01–R06).

11
Phase IV: Computational Analysis

Risk Classification & Scoring

Applied standard Likelihood × Severity Risk Matrix protocol to assign risk scores from 1 to 25 and classify each hotspot as High, Medium, or Low Risk.

12
Phase V: Civic Tech Platform

Digital GIS Risk Mapping

Developed an interactive Leaflet.js + OpenStreetMap application displaying georeferenced hazard pins, photo evidence, and dynamic filters.

13
Phase V: Civic Tech Platform

Remedial Proposals Formulation

Created 4-pillar engineering, speed control, policy, and educational solutions mapped to IRC 67/99 school-zone safety guidelines.

14
Phase VI: Academic Deliverables

Comprehensive Project Documentation

Compiled detailed technical report, empirical survey appendices, photo logs, and step-by-step data replacement guides in README.md.

15
Phase VI: Academic Deliverables

Viva Voce Examination & Demonstration

Defending the CEP project before university examiners with live demonstrations of interactive GIS maps, Chart.js analytics, and survey logging.

Risk Assessment Matrix

Hazard Severity & Likelihood Matrix

Standard 5×5 scoring model utilized to categorize conflict zones into High, Medium, and Low Risk categories.

Likelihood / Probability Negligible (1) Minor (2) Moderate (3) Severe (4) Catastrophic (5)
Almost Certain (5) 5 (Med) 10 (Med) 15 (High) 20 (High) 25 (High)
Likely / Frequent (4) 4 (Low) 8 (Med) 12 (Med) 16 (High) 20 (High)
Possible (3) 3 (Low) 6 (Low) 9 (Med) 12 (Med) 15 (High)
Unlikely (2) 2 (Low) 4 (Low) 6 (Low) 8 (Med) 10 (Med)
Rare (1) 1 (Low) 2 (Low) 3 (Low) 4 (Low) 5 (Med)
Score 15–25: High Risk (Immediate intervention needed)
Score 8–14: Medium Risk (Short-term mitigation)
Score 1–7: Low Risk (Routine maintenance)
